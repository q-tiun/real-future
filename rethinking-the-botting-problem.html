An Insider's Take

Rethinking the Botting Problem

Been paying attention to the recent conversation around botting, and one thing became pretty clear: Real may be looking at this problem very differently than most users.
— Banning botters isn't as simple as it sounds

A lot of users keep asking the same thing: why doesn't Real just ban botters?

Based on recent dev comments, the issue isn't that simple. Many users assume botters should be easy to detect based on offer timing, but the devs pushed back on that idea. They explained that thousands of chronically online users already show very similar behavior patterns, making it much harder to reliably distinguish automation from highly active real users.

Even if bot detection improves, it was made clear that this isn't something that's going to be solved through a few Discord messages.

Botting Detection
— The real fix may be changing incentives

What stood out most from the discussion is that the focus doesn't seem to be on eliminating botters entirely. Instead, the goal appears to be removing the advantage of botting in the first place.

As one of the devs explained, the only real way to prevent botting is to change the incentives so there's no benefit to it. That shifts the conversation away from bans and toward marketplace design.

Right now, the biggest issue seems to be pack feed sniping. If a valuable card gets pulled, being the first person to send an offer creates a major advantage. That's where botting becomes valuable.

Marketplace Incentives
— Auctions could change everything

One solution discussed was moving non-player pack cards to auctions instead of instant swaps. That would completely change the current dynamic.

Instead of racing to be first, users would simply bid during a short auction window. If two or more people want the same card, they bid. Simple.

That removes the main incentive for bots camping the pack feed because speed matters much less in an auction system. The devs even suggested this would completely solve the current pack feed issue.

Auctions Pack Feed
— Listing changes may help too

Another interesting idea involved reducing listing friction. Users suggested removing the 10 Rax listing fee for cards listed shortly after being pulled.

The idea of removing the fee within the first 10 minutes after opening a pack was well received. That could encourage significantly more listings, putting more cards on auction and reducing dependence on instant offers.

The devs also mentioned a new feature is in the works that would let users quickly list cards directly from the pack screen with no listing fee.

Listings Rax Fee
— Instant transfers may still have a place

Instant transfers also came up during the discussion. One idea was limiting instant-accept offers to player packs so swaps can still exist without affecting the pack feed.

Another suggestion was allowing instant transfers after 24 hours if a card still hasn't sold, giving newer cards the protection of auctions while allowing older listings to move more freely. Both ideas received positive feedback.

Instant Transfers Player Packs
— Bigger picture

The biggest takeaway here is that Real may not be trying to win a war against botters directly. Instead, the focus seems to be on redesigning the marketplace so botting becomes far less useful.

That's a much bigger shift than simply banning accounts, and if these ideas make it into the app, the marketplace could look very different.
