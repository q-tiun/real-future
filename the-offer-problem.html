An Insider’s Take

The Offer Problem

Been paying attention to the conversation around offers lately, and there’s clearly more going on than just users complaining about botters.
— Botting is still a real issue

A lot of users keep asking the same thing: why doesn’t Real just ban botters?

The issue is that it’s not always easy to tell the difference between automation and someone who’s just chronically online sending hundreds of offers a day.

Even when accounts get banned, alt accounts can replace them quickly, making enforcement harder than people think.

Botting Ongoing Issue
— Offer spam became the meta, and that’s the real problem

Botting is still a real issue, and a lot of users are frustrated by how much it affects the marketplace.

But based on dev comments, the deeper problem goes beyond just bots.

The bigger issue is that users feel like they need to spam offers. If the most effective strategy is sending hundreds of offers and hoping one gets accepted, that points to friction somewhere in the system — whether it’s pricing, listing visibility, or how offers are handled.

One idea that stood out recently focuses on listings. Right now, the 10 Rax listing fee discourages people from putting cards on the market, which naturally leads to fewer listings and more reliance on offers.

A possible fix is automatic re-listing. If a card doesn’t sell, it could automatically re-list for free at a slightly lower price — say 10 Rax below the previous listing. For example, if a Rare Pass gets listed at 300 and doesn’t sell, it could automatically re-list at 290 without charging another fee.

That would populate the market much more and help cards find fair value faster. A dev even said this was a good idea, along with removing listing fees for those same cards.

The devs have said they’re working on changes to make offers less necessary. If those updates land, the goal won’t just be reducing botting — it’ll be making offer spam a weaker strategy in the first place.
